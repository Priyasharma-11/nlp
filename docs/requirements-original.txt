Before every major phase:

* Explain the objective.
* Explain the planned implementation.
* Identify files and modules affected.
* Identify new dependencies.
* Identify potential risks.
* State expected deliverables.
* Request my approval.

Do not proceed with a major architectural or dependency change until approval is received.
Routine debugging and small implementation corrections within an approved phase may proceed independently.
Beginner-Friendly Development
I am developing this project individually and want to understand its implementation.
Therefore:

* Explain important technical decisions.
* Explain why each library is being used.
* Explain the purpose of each major file.
* Provide exact commands.
* Specify where code must be placed.
* Explain how to run and verify functionality.
* Explain common errors and their solutions.
* Avoid unnecessary assumptions about my technical experience.

Do not simply dump large amounts of code without explaining the implementation.
Maintain a balance between autonomous execution and understandable step-by-step development.
Strict Quality Rules

* Do not stop after creating a project plan.
* Do not stop after generating UI screens.
* Do not stop after writing isolated backend functions.
* Do not deliver a non-functional prototype.
* Do not simulate successful API calls.
* Do not use fake data without clearly labelling it.
* Do not invent scientific papers or citations.
* Do not fabricate model performance.
* Do not leave critical functionality as TODO comments.
* Do not claim a feature is complete without verification.
* Do not claim tests passed unless they were actually executed.
* Do not install unnecessary dependencies.
* Do not expose secrets.
* Do not sacrifice maintainability for unnecessary complexity.

If a blocker occurs, investigate alternatives and explain the issue.
Maintain ownership of the complete project lifecycle.
